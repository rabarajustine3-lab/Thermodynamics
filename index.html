What am I looking at?

In the particle view, blue particles are solid (vibrating in a lattice), teal are liquid (sliding past each other), orange are gas (fast, filling the container). During melting and boiling, added heat converts particles one by one; their speed, and so the temperature, does not rise until the change finishes. Faster particles mean higher temperature (kinetic theory).

Glossary

Sensible heat

Heat that changes the temperature of a substance without changing its phase: Q = mcΔT.

Conduction, convection and radiation

Conduction passes energy between neighbouring particles. Convection carries it with a moving fluid. Radiation carries it as electromagnetic waves and can cross a vacuum.

Kinetic theory of gases

Gas particles move randomly. Their average translational kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature: (3/2)kBT.

Specific heat capacity (c)

Heat needed to raise 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. Water is high (4.186 kJ/kg·K), which is why it heats slowly.

Latent heat (L)

Heat per kilogram needed to change phase with no temperature change. Vaporization needs about 7 times more than melting for water.

Internal energy (U)

Total microscopic kinetic and potential energy of the particles. For a monatomic ideal gas, U = (3/2)nRT.

Work (W) and sign convention

Here W is work done by the gas. Expansion gives W > 0 and uses up energy; compression gives W < 0.

Adiabatic process

No heat crosses the boundary (Q = 0). Compressing heats the gas; expanding cools it.
